Adult Social Care Outcomes
Kent & Medway

ASCOF measures what the current care system achieves for people already within it. This page pairs that accountability data with Assistiv's upstream FEP intelligence — showing not just where outcomes are today, but where unmet need is concentrating before it reaches the system.

📊
What ASCOF measures
Annual published scores for each council area — covering quality of life, independence, experience of care, and safety. It tells commissioners how the current system is performing on the people already in it.
⚠️
What ASCOF misses
People who haven't yet entered formal care — the Missing Middle — don't appear in ASCOF denominators. Their outcomes aren't measured. Their needs aren't counted. They are the population Assistiv is designed to reach.
🔗
Why this matters here
Pairing ASCOF performance with FEP district risk closes the loop: current outcomes + upstream risk = commissioning argument. Where outcomes are weak and FEP is high, the case for early intervention is strongest.

💡

The commissioning argument in plain terms: Kent's ASCOF performance is uneven — strong on reablement (2D1) but below average on quality of life, social contact and satisfaction. These aren't independent failures. They reflect a system that intervenes late, at crisis point, rather than earlier when outcomes — and costs — are more favourable.

Assistiv's FEP intelligence shows that the highest-risk districts (Thanet FEP 77, Folkestone & Hythe FEP 50, Dover FEP 46) are also the areas where social isolation, deprivation and health pressures converge. These are the places where ASCOF performance is most likely to deteriorate further — and where earlier, person-led contact produces the greatest return. The gap between what ASCOF measures and what Assistiv can see upstream is where the investment case lives.

How ASCOT Connects ASCOF to Individual Outcomes
Population level
↓
ASCOF 1A: Quality of Life Score
Kent: 18.7 · England: 19.1
Annual published figure for the whole CASSR. Tells commissioners how the system is performing but not where, or for whom, outcomes are weakest.
Individual screening level
↓
ASCOT-SCT4: Person-level quality of life
Eight domains · Control · Safety · Social participation
Assistiv uses ASCOT questions in its screening tool. Responses from individuals map directly onto the same domains measured by ASCOF — enabling sub-CASSR aggregation by district, cohort, or risk band.
Upstream risk level
↓
FEP: Frailty Emergence Probability
District-level · 0–100 scale · Live signal
FEP identifies where demand is concentrating before it enters the care system. Districts with high FEP and weak ASCOF performance are the natural target for preventative commissioning.
⚠️

The Missing Middle gap: ASCOF denominators include only people who are already in contact with adult social care services. The 78% of older people living independently — including those Assistiv screens — are invisible to ASCOF. Their quality of life, social contact and sense of control are not counted. This means ASCOF systematically underestimates the scale of unmet need in the community, particularly in high-FEP coastal districts where service uptake is lowest relative to need.
